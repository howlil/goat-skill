# Visual composition

Follow the explicit brief, project design or brand guidance, established tokens and components, then comparable product surfaces. Code supplies the actual token values. Surface contradictions that would materially change a decision. Extend the established visual system only where it cannot express the task.

Start with what the consumer needs to notice or do first. Map priority to reading and focus order, then group related information by proximity and alignment. Use a border or container when the boundary conveys a separate state, interaction, or scroll region. Make spacing express the strength of relationships. Choose density from task frequency and information load. Frequent comparisons may benefit from a compact, aligned view. Render actual content and fix hierarchy and alignment before polishing.

When a new direction is needed, tie the consequential choices to the product's task or identity. If exchanging the logo and copy would leave an equally plausible surface for an unrelated product, revisit a choice such as information hierarchy, density, or imagery. Preserve intentional conventions. Concentrate any strong visual expression where it supports the primary task.

At a constrained width, decide which region keeps its place, reflows, discloses detail, or scrolls. Keep the primary task and important controls reachable with a coherent reading and focus order. Use native layout mechanics for wrapping, shrinking, and scrolling before adding measurement.

Derive stress inputs from the schema, validation limits, representative data, and supported environments. Vary the likeliest risk first, such as a long valid name, an absent field, many results, or the narrowest supported container. Combine conditions where they interact. Check for hidden actions, overlap, misleading truncation, or focus order that diverges from visual order. Fix the structural cause across valid inputs. Apply the same method to supported zoom, localization, direction, or themes when the change can affect them.

Composition and stress guidance draws on [Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable/blob/main/skill/reference/layout.md), [Frontend Design](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md), [Break UI](https://github.com/emilkowalski/skills/blob/main/skills/break-ui/SKILL.md), and [UI Design](https://github.com/mblode/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/ui-design/SKILL.md).
