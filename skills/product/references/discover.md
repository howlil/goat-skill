# Discovery

Start with the decision the evidence could change. Rule out options that violate binding constraints. Focus on the uncertainty that decides whether the intended consumer needs the outcome, can use it, or can get it within real constraints. Check another risk when it could change the decision or rule out an alternative.

Use evidence suited to the claim. Usage, support, and consumer reports can show need. Code and prototypes can demonstrate behavior or feasibility within their test conditions. Feasibility alone cannot show demand. State what the strongest evidence supports, what remains an assumption, and what observation would change the recommendation.

When evidence is worth gathering, use the smallest authorized probe with a clear result criterion. Define its scope and say whether it ran. Production exposure, instrumentation, spending, or data collection requires authority for those effects. A recommendation does not grant execution permission.

A personal preference or a deliberate exploratory build may justify scoped work without market evidence. Keep experiments and exploratory work within the user's request and binding constraints. Match any production readiness claim to evidence for that scope.
